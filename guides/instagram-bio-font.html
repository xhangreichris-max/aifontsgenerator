How to Change the Font in Your Instagram Bio

By Christopher Shangrei, Chrismark Studio · Published 22 November 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer: Instagram's help center doesn't describe a font menu for your bio, so the usual way to change the font is to copy styled text from a font generator and paste it into the Bio field. Open your profile, tap Edit profile, tap Bio, paste, and save. Styled letters are special Unicode characters rather than a real font, so check how your bio looks after saving.

Instagram does offer built-in font choices in one spot. Its help center says that when you add a song with lyrics to a story, you can choose different fonts and designs for the lyrics. It doesn't mention anything like that for bios.

How to Change Your Instagram Bio Font, Step by Step

First, make your styled text. Type your bio line into a generator such as the main generator on our homepage, pick a style, and copy it. Then paste it into Instagram using the steps from Instagram's help center. Button labels can differ between app versions.

On iPhone or Android:

  1. Tap Profile or your profile picture in the bottom right.
  2. Tap Edit profile at the top of the screen.
  3. Tap Bio, then paste or type your text.
  4. Tap Done on iPhone, or the save icon on Android.

On a computer:

  1. Click your profile picture on the left to open your profile.
  2. Click Edit profile, then click the text box next to Bio.
  3. Paste your text.
  4. Click Submit to save it.

Instagram allows a bio of up to 150 characters, and anyone can see it. After saving, look at your profile on your own phone and, if you can, on someone else's device too.

Your name and username live on the same Edit profile screen, and Instagram also lets you change them in Accounts Center under Profiles. If your account reaches a lot of people, Instagram says a username change may need to be reviewed, and your followers may be told about it.

Name, Bio or Captions: Where Fancy Fonts Fit

Your profile has several kinds of text, and styled letters suit some of them far better than others.

  • Username. Keep it plain. Instagram's help center doesn't publish a full list of allowed characters, but when a username is taken, its advice is to add periods, numbers, underscores or abbreviations. It doesn't suggest anything fancier, and people type your username to find and tag you.
  • Name. This is the bold line under your picture. Instagram says using the same first and last name on linked Facebook and Instagram accounts can help people find you through recommendations. A fully styled name won't look like the plain name people search for, so keep your real name in ordinary letters and add style around it, if at all.
  • Bio. The best place for styled text. Use it for one line or a short label, like a heading above your links, and leave the rest plain so it stays easy to read.
  • Captions and comments. A styled title at the top of a caption works well. Long styled paragraphs are tiring to read and cause problems for screen readers, as covered below.

On how often you can rename yourself, Instagram's help center only gives a limit for Meta Verified profiles, which can update their profile name twice every 14 days. It doesn't state a limit for other accounts, so check what the app allows.

  • Code points: the number of Unicode characters.
  • UTF-16 units: how many 16-bit pieces the text needs in UTF-16, the text format used inside JavaScript, Java and Windows.
  • UTF-8 bytes: the size of the text in UTF-8, the usual format on the web.

The math-alphabet styles (bold, cursive bold, gothic and double struck) keep the same 29 characters, but every styled letter needs 2 UTF-16 units instead of 1, and 4 UTF-8 bytes instead of 1. Gothic and double struck come out one unit lower because their capital C is an older single-unit character, ℭ or ℂ. Small caps stays at 29 units because its letters are single-unit characters, and you can make it with the Small Caps card in the Unicode styles section of our chicano font generator. Tiny adds 8 characters of its own, because the style wraps the line in ₊˚ʚ decorations.

Instagram says the bio limit is 150 characters, but it doesn't publish how it counts styled characters. Depending on how the app counts, styled text may fill your 150 faster than plain text. If Instagram cuts your bio short or won't save it, style fewer words.

An easy way to stay inside the limit is to write the whole bio in plain text first and make sure it fits. Then swap just one line for its styled version. If that line uses a math-alphabet style, leave a little spare room, since it's the part most likely to count for more than it looks.

What Can Break When Other People See It

Three things can go wrong after you paste styled text, and you won't see any of them on your own screen.

  • Boxes. A phone with no font for a character shows an empty box in its place. Our guide on why styled text becomes boxes explains the cause and what to do about it.
  • Search. Styled letters are different characters from plain ones. Someone searching for coffee types plain letters, so a word you styled may not match.
  • Screen readers. A screen reader may read styled letters one at a time, read out their long Unicode names, or skip them. Our guide on how screen readers cope with fancy fonts covers what listeners actually hear.

The fix for all three is the same: style a word or one short line, not the whole bio, and keep the important words plain.

Spacing and Line Breaks in Your Bio

Fonts are only half of how a bio looks. The other half is spacing: line breaks, empty lines and gaps between sections. Instagram's help center doesn't explain how it handles extra spaces or empty lines, so look at your profile once the bio is saved. For blank lines and spacer characters, our guide to invisible names and blank spaces has characters you can copy and explains why some of them get trimmed.
